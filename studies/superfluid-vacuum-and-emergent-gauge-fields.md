# Superfluid vacuum: Volovik's ³He program, emergent gauge fields, and two-fluid wave content

The serious modern version of "the vacuum is a condensed medium and particles are its
excitations": Volovik's superfluid-³He analog program. This note carries what that program
actually establishes — because it is the standard any new substrate ontology must be
measured against — plus the generic wave content of a *two*-component medium. Both bind the
two-substance vortex vacuum in
[theory/two-substance-vortex-vacuum](../theory/two-substance-vortex-vacuum.md) (ORB-10160).

## The Volovik program (³He-A as a vacuum analog)

Reference: Volovik, *The Universe in a Helium Droplet* (Oxford University Press,
International Series of Monographs on Physics 117, 2003). Load-bearing results:

- **Emergent Weyl fermions.** Superfluid ³He-A has gapless fermionic quasiparticles at
  isolated points of momentum space (Fermi points). Near a Fermi point the quasiparticle
  spectrum is *forced* into the relativistic form g^{μν}(p−qA)_μ(p−qA)_ν = 0 — massless
  chiral (Weyl) fermions, though the underlying atoms are non-relativistic.
- **Emergent gauge fields and metric.** In that expansion, the slow collective motions of
  the order parameter enter *exactly* as an effective gauge field A_μ and an effective
  metric g^{μν} (vierbein) acting on the quasiparticles. Electrodynamics-like and
  gravity-like fields appear as low-energy collective modes of the vacuum medium, not as
  added ingredients.
- **Topological protection (the universality-class theorem).** The Fermi point carries a
  momentum-space topological invariant; its existence and the relativistic form of the
  low-energy action are stable against perturbations of the microphysics. This is the
  crucial logical structure: the emergence of one shared light cone, chiral fermions, and
  gauge fields near a Fermi point is a *theorem about a universality class*, not a tuning.
  Any medium in the class delivers it; a medium outside the class generically does not.
- **Emergent Lorentz invariance is low-energy only.** The invariance holds near the Fermi
  point and breaks at the medium's microscopic scale — appearing as high-energy dispersion
  (the bound side of this lives in
  [lorentz-violation-bounds](lorentz-violation-bounds.md)).
- **What the program does *not* deliver.** The effective metric's *dynamics* is not
  Einstein's: the medium obeys its own hydrodynamics, and the induced gravitational action
  is contaminated by non-covariant terms (the same caution as
  [river-model-and-analog-gravity](river-model-and-analog-gravity.md) — analog models
  reproduce propagation on a metric, not the Einstein equations for it). Relatedly, the
  equivalence principle is a known open difficulty for superfluid-vacuum theories:
  quasiparticle energy, not constituent count, must be the only gravitating quantity, and
  that has to be arranged, not assumed.

The one-sentence standard this sets: **in a substrate theory, "one light, one cone, gauge
coupling universal" is something you prove from the topology of the vacuum, or you do not
have it.**

## Two-fluid media carry two sounds

The generic wave content of a medium with two components (established physics, He II):

- **Landau's two-fluid model** (Landau 1941, *J. Phys. USSR* 5, 71): He II described as
  interpenetrating superfluid and normal components with independent velocity fields.
- **Two propagating scalar branches**: first sound (components in phase — an ordinary
  density/pressure wave) and second sound (components in counterflow — an
  entropy/temperature wave), predicted by the two-fluid equations and measured by Peshkov
  (1944, *J. Phys. USSR* 8, 381; second sound speed ~20 m/s vs ~240 m/s first sound —
  grossly different speeds).
- **The generic lesson**: each relative degree of freedom of a multi-component medium is
  an extra propagating branch with its own speed. Two components → two scalar sounds is
  the default; a *single* shared propagation speed is the exception that requires a
  mechanism (a locking symmetry, or Fermi-point-type protection as above).
