# Vortex atoms, vortex media, and quantized circulation

The canonical lineage of "particles are vortices in a substrate" — who tried it, why, what
killed each attempt, and which piece of it became real measured physics (quantized
circulation). This is the family the two-substance vortex vacuum is placed against in
[theory/two-substance-vortex-vacuum](../theory/two-substance-vortex-vacuum.md) (ORB-10160).

## Kelvin's vortex atoms and the knot-theory origin

- **Helmholtz 1858** ("Über Integrale der hydrodynamischen Gleichungen, welche den
  Wirbelbewegungen entsprechen", *J. reine angew. Math.* 55, 25–55): in an ideal
  (inviscid, barotropic) fluid, vortex lines are permanent — they cannot be created,
  destroyed, or cut; circulation is conserved (as later formalized by Kelvin's circulation
  theorem). The permanence result is what made vortices a candidate for matter.
- **Kelvin (W. Thomson) 1867** ("On Vortex Atoms", *Proc. R. Soc. Edinburgh* 6, 94–105;
  reprinted *Phil. Mag.* 34, 15–24): atoms as knotted/linked vortex rings in the aether.
  The motivation is exactly the identity-of-particles observation — Helmholtz permanence
  gives indestructible atoms, and the *topological type* of the knot gives a discrete
  species label, explaining why every atom of an element is exactly alike, where lumps of
  "hard matter" could vary continuously.
- **Tait's knot tables**: to enumerate the possible "elements", Tait began classifying
  knots (the "On Knots" series, *Trans. R. Soc. Edinburgh*, 1877–1885) — knot theory as a
  mathematical discipline is a direct descendant of the vortex-atom program.
- **How it died**: no quantitative derivation of spectra or chemistry ever emerged; vortex
  rings in an ideal fluid were later shown to have stability problems; and the carrier
  medium itself died with the aether
  ([lorentz-violation-bounds](lorentz-violation-bounds.md)). The *motivation* was
  vindicated by a different mechanism: in quantum field theory identical particles are
  identical because they are quanta of one field — identity comes from a shared substrate
  after all, but the discrete label is the field, not a knot.

## Maxwell's vortex cells and the removability lesson

- **Maxwell 1861–62** ("On Physical Lines of Force", *Phil. Mag.* ser. 4, vols. 21 & 23,
  in four parts): the EM field as a mechanical medium of molecular vortices (magnetic
  field = vortex angular velocity) separated by "idle wheel" electric particles. The
  displacement current — the term that completes the field equations and predicts EM waves
  — was found *inside this mechanical model*, from the elasticity of the vortex cells.
- **Maxwell 1865** ("A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field", *Phil. Trans. R.
  Soc.* 155, 459–512): the same field equations, restated with the vortex machinery
  entirely removed — the fields and their energy became the primitive objects.
- **The lesson** (load-bearing for any substrate proposal): the mechanical substrate was
  *removable* — everything it predicted survived in the equations, nothing it added was
  ever measured. A substrate model of EM must either reproduce Maxwell exactly and predict
  something beyond it, or Occam removes the machinery exactly as Maxwell himself did.

## Bjerknes forces: the hydrodynamic sign reversal

- **C. A. Bjerknes's pulsating-sphere analogies**, developed from the 1870s and presented
  systematically in V. Bjerknes, *Fields of Force* (Columbia University Press, 1906):
  bodies pulsating **in phase attract; in antiphase they repel** — and the same holds for
  the modern "secondary Bjerknes force" between oscillating bubbles. Translated to the
  electrostatic analogy this is *like charges attract* — hydrodynamic monopole analogies
  generically come out with the **reverse of the electrostatic sign rule**.
- The field-theory face of the same fact: exchange of an even-spin (scalar) mediator makes
  like charges attract; only odd-spin (vector) exchange makes like charges repel and
  opposites attract (textbook treatment: Zee, *Quantum Field Theory in a Nutshell*, 2nd
  ed., Princeton 2010, §I.5).
- **What this binds**: any medium model of EM owes a demonstration that its like-sign
  defects *repel* — the sign is not free, and the canonical hydrodynamic precedent gets it
  wrong. A circulation (vector-shaped) coupling is the structurally motivated escape; a
  density/pressure (scalar-shaped) coupling is expected to fail.

## Quantized circulation — the piece that became real physics

- **Onsager 1949** ("Statistical hydrodynamics", *Nuovo Cimento* 6, Suppl. 2, 279–287,
  the Florence IUPAP remark): circulation in a superfluid is quantized in units of
  **κ = h/m**. **Feynman 1955** ("Application of Quantum Mechanics to Liquid Helium",
  *Progress in Low Temperature Physics* vol. 1, ch. 2, 17–53) reached quantized vortex
  lines independently and built the phenomenology of superfluid turbulence on them.
- **Measured**: Vinen 1961 (*Proc. R. Soc. A* 260, 218–236) observed the circulation
  quantum h/m on a fine wire in rotating He II. The superconducting face — flux
  quantization in units h/2e through Abrikosov vortices (Abrikosov 1957, *Sov. Phys. JETP*
  5, 1174) — was measured the same year (Deaver & Fairbank, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 7, 43
  (1961); Doll & Näbauer, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 7, 51 (1961)).
- **Winding is exact; core energy is dynamical.** The quantization is topological — the
  order-parameter phase must return to itself mod 2π around the vortex — so it is *exactly*
  integer regardless of core structure, temperature, or history; the core size and energy
  are set by dynamics (healing length) and are not quantized. This split is measured
  physics, not speculation.
- **Multiply-wound vortices split.** Vortex energy scales roughly as winding², so a
  doubly-quantized vortex is unstable to splitting into two singly-quantized ones —
  observed directly in Bose–Einstein condensates (Shin et al. 2004, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 93,
  160406, dynamical instability of a doubly quantized vortex; vortex *lattices* of unit
  vortices: Madison et al. 2000, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 84, 806). A "multiplicity-m particle"
  in a vortex model is therefore generically a *bound set of m unit vortices*, not one
  m-wound core.
- **Fractional vortices are confined.** In two-component condensates a vortex can wind in
  one component only, carrying a fraction of the flux quantum; such fractional vortices
  are bound by a linear-tension string to partners and cannot exist in isolation — only
  integer-summing composites are free (Babaev 2002, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 89, 067001, for
  two-gap superconductors / the extended Faddeev model).

## The nuclear-electron lesson (composite-neutron models)

Before the neutron's discovery (Chadwick 1932), nuclei were modeled as protons plus bound
"nuclear electrons" — N-14 would contain 14 protons + 7 electrons, an odd number of
fermions, hence Fermi statistics. Rasetti's rotational Raman spectra of N₂ (1929) showed
the N-14 nucleus obeys **Bose** statistics; Heitler & Herzberg 1929 ("Gehorchen die
Stickstoffkerne der Boseschen Statistik?", *Naturwissenschaften* 17, 673) drew the
conclusion that the nuclear-electron picture contradicts the measured statistics. The
composite neutron (proton + electron) was dead before the neutron was found. **What this
binds**: a particle model that builds the neutron as a bound (proton + electron) pair
inherits a measured statistics contradiction; the neutron must be as elementary as the
proton, with its own spin-½.
