# Gravitational redshift at astronomical scales

The laboratory-to-orbit clock ladder lives in
[gravitational-time-dilation](gravitational-time-dilation.md); this note carries the rungs
*beyond* the solar neighborhood — the Sun's surface, a star at the Galactic-center black
hole, and stacked galaxy clusters. These are the instruments for any theory in which clock
rates could decouple from the dynamically inferred potential at astronomical scales (the
S2-disjunction confrontation in
[theory/two-substance-vortex-vacuum](../theory/two-substance-vortex-vacuum.md), ORB-10161).

## The measured rungs

| System | Scale | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solar photospheric Fe lines (Moon-reflected sunlight, HARPS + laser frequency comb) | 1 AU | measured 638 ± 6 m/s (line-by-line; 639 ± 14 m/s from the line-list average) vs the GR prediction 633.1 m/s for light received at Earth | González Hernández et al. 2020, *Astron. Astrophys.* 643, A146 |
| S2/S0-2 pericenter passage at Sgr A* (~2800 gravitational radii of a 4 × 10⁶ M☉ black hole) | ~10⁻³ pc | redshift parameter f = 0.90 ± 0.09 (stat) ± 0.15 (sys) at the 2018 passage (f = 0 Newtonian, f = 1 GR); improved to f = 1.04 ± 0.05 (20σ from Newtonian); independent confirmation by the UCLA group | GRAVITY Collaboration 2018, *Astron. Astrophys.* 615, L15; GRAVITY Collaboration 2019, *Astron. Astrophys.* 625, L10; Do et al. 2019, *Science* 365, 664 |
| Stacked galaxy clusters (7,800 SDSS clusters; member-galaxy spectra vs brightest cluster galaxy) | ~1 Mpc | net gravitational offset of ~10 km/s magnitude between cluster members and the BCG at the potential minimum, detected at 99% confidence, consistent with the GR prediction for the *dynamically inferred* (dark-matter-dominated) cluster potential | Wojtak, Hansen & Hjorth 2011, *Nature* 477, 567 |

**Caveat on the cluster rung.** Kaiser 2013 (*Mon. Not. R. Astron. Soc.* 435, 1278) showed
that kinematic terms of comparable size (transverse Doppler shift, light-cone and
surface-brightness selection effects) contribute to the stacked signal and must be modeled;
the measured magnitude has held up in later analyses, but its weight as a *precision* GR test
is limited at the O(1) level. It remains the only rung where the dynamically inferred and
baryons-only potentials differ enough (a factor of several in mass) for a clock measurement
to distinguish them.

## What the rungs establish, structurally

1. **Redshift tracks the dynamics-determined potential at every measured scale.** The solar
   rung uses the ephemeris GM☉; the S2 rung's potential is fixed by the same orbit that
   exhibits the redshift; the cluster rung's prediction uses the halo mass inferred from
   member kinematics/lensing — and each measurement agrees. Any theory in which clock rates
   read a *different* potential than orbits do (e.g. a baryons-only potential while a fifth
   force carries the missing dynamics) predicts a specific deficit on the cluster rung:
   roughly the baryon fraction of the signal, ~5× below the measured ~10 km/s — disfavored
   by Wojtak et al. at the ~2σ level (soft, given the Kaiser caveat).
2. **The S2 rung is black-hole-dominated.** At ~10⁻³ pc the potential is that of the central
   mass; kpc-scale structure in the Galaxy's field contributes nothing measurable to S2's
   redshift. It anchors redshift-tracks-dynamics in a strong(er)-field regime; it does not
   probe galactic-scale clock anomalies.
3. **No current instrument reads clock rates across kpc baselines at interesting precision.**
   Between the solar rung (m/s at 1 AU) and the cluster rung (km/s at Mpc), a systematic
   radial redshift trend across the Galactic disk of order tens of m/s would sit below
   current stellar radial-velocity systematics; the potential differences at issue are
   ~10⁻⁷–10⁻⁶ in z. A theory predicting kpc-scale clock anomalies at that level is currently
   unconstrained by direct measurement — the binding constraints come through the solar-system
   profile tests instead (see the theory doc's stiffness computation).
