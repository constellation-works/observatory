What is being counted?

Each dot is one event in a seeded Poisson process, at density ρ = 64 per unit spacetime area. A causal chain can step only inside or on the future light cone. The highlighted polyline is an actual longest chain, found by sorting in null coordinates. In a dense 1+1 sprinkling its length approaches τ√(2ρ); one finite count fluctuates. Events exactly at the endpoint markers are excluded.

Twin mode fixes both endpoints and forces the orange chain through a virtual turnaround, adding the two segment chains. It cannot exceed the unconstrained cyan chain, but ties can occur. Even at v = 0, forcing a waypoint can reduce a finite sample’s count. This is a discrete path proxy, not events lying exactly on a smooth worldline.

The rod never changes in its rest frame. The slab has fixed thickness Δt′ in its own observer’s time, so its rest-frame thickness is Δt′√(1 − w²). The lower edge meets the ends at different times. Counting its intersection with the worldtube estimates ℓ√(1 − w²); this is a volume-based estimator, not a spatial-distance reconstruction or a camera photograph.

A boost transforms every event and geometric layer with x′ = γ(x − vt), t′ = γ(t − vx). The viewport stays fixed; offscreen events come from the same deterministic, extended sprinkling. Counts of the selected regions do not change just because you change view. Statistical Lorentz invariance concerns the Poisson distribution, not an identical dot pattern. The lattice exposes a preferred frame, although its causal order also survives a coordinate transformation.

Minkowski geometry and its light cones are inputs to this model. The animation illustrates their consequences; it does not derive relativity from discreteness. See the R014 derivation and numerical experiment and Discreteness without symmetry breaking.
