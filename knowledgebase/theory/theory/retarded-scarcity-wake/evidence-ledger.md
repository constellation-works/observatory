| Under the delayed-center postulate, uniform translation produces a first-order potential dipole proportional to \(v/c_g\), with higher conventional potential on the leading edge and stronger attraction on the trailing edge | untested | The algebra is internally consistent, but \(\Phi_{\rm lag}\) is not the compact-source exterior solution of the standard Poisson equation: it has \(\nabla^2\Phi\neq0\) outside the galaxy and a linearly divergent Poisson-equivalent dipole moment. The analytic check awaits a cataloged negative-control fixture — [moving-source-field-consistency](../moving-source-field-consistency/) |
| The first-order acceleration contrast is \((g_{\rm trail}-g_{\rm lead})/g_0\simeq4\beta_g\) | untested | Analytically corrected to \(2\beta_g\) in § Leading/trailing sign. \(4\beta_g\) follows from a *different*, non-potential force rule than the declared \(\mathbf{a}=-\nabla\Phi\); the fixture remains to be cataloged — [moving-source-field-consistency](../moving-source-field-consistency/) |
| The alternative "force points toward the retarded position" rule is a legitimate conservative mechanic | untested | Analytically it has nonzero curl and admits loop-dependent work \(\sim\beta_g GM/R\); constant-\(R\) circles return zero, so the fixture must vary eccentricity and apsidal orientation — [moving-source-field-consistency](../moving-source-field-consistency/) |
| A centrally concentrated galaxy preserves a larger coherent wake than a diffuse distribution with the same total mass | refuted | Moot for the rejected delayed-center shortcut, and false for the explicit Branch C equation: at first order the exterior dipole of the dissipative term is set by the far field of the Newtonian potential, i.e. by total mass alone, so \(A_1(r)\to\beta_g r/2\ell_\Gamma\) independent of central concentration up to \(O(a/r)\); the source-delay term is a rigid shift of the whole potential, likewise concentration-blind — [Branch C](../moving-source-field-consistency/branch-c.md) § The first-order moving-source solution (ORB-11175) |
| The wake displaces the dynamical center behind the baryonic center and produces an \(m=1\) leading/trailing rotation asymmetry rather than an axisymmetric flat-curve boost | refuted | Refuted on both counts by the explicit equation (ORB-11175, [Branch C](../moving-source-field-consistency/branch-c.md)): the dissipative solution's first-order leading/trailing *radial* contrast vanishes (the first-order force is tangential, \(g_0A_1\sin\theta\), zero on the leading/trailing axis), and the amplitude itself is capped by the solar-system self-acceleration wall at \(A_1(10\,{\rm kpc})\lesssim10^{-3}\)–\(3\times10^{-5}\), two to three orders below observed lopsidedness ([solar-system-ephemeris-precision-floor](../../studies/solar-system-ephemeris-precision-floor.md)). The source-delay term displaces the centre, but by a fixed \(\lvert\tau_s\rvert w<R_\odot\), unobservable at galactic radii |
| A correlation between observed asymmetry axes and a common inferred substrate velocity would be evidence for the wake (§ Observable signatures, last bullet; rejection gate 6) | untested — moot: Branch C closed | Moot: Branch C closed (ORB-11175). The conditional stands as logic, but no surviving model predicts an amplitude above \(10^{-3}\), and the cosmological baseline it would have needed — the leading tidal tensor is quadrupolar and does not by itself establish an \(m=1\) phase correlation ([moving-source-field-consistency](../moving-source-field-consistency/) § A degeneracy to measure, not assume) — is no longer owed by this family |
| A boost-violating model may produce a lopsidedness-versus-vertical-distortion split set partly by the angle between \(\mathbf{w}\) and the disk normal | untested — moot: Branch C closed | Moot: Branch C closed (ORB-11175). In the minimal equation the split exists in form — in-plane \(\mathbf{w}\) gives the tangential \(m=1\) force, perpendicular \(\mathbf{w}\) a \(1/R\) midplane vertical acceleration (bowl-like, not an integral-sign warp) — at amplitudes the ephemeris floor caps below \(10^{-3}\) ([Branch C](../moving-source-field-consistency/branch-c.md)) |
| A specified boost-invariant causal field can cancel the delayed-center model's first-order dipole while retaining finite propagation speed | supported | Previously conjecture pending the local study note; supported 2026-08-28 by [moving-source-gravity-bounds](../../studies/moving-source-gravity-bounds.md) § aberration cancellation (Heaviside/Liénard–Wiechert exact for uniform motion; Carlip 2000 through \(O((v/c)^5)\) radiation-reaction order in general relativity), and reproduced inside the Branch C equation with its time-odd sector off — the Heaviside form, even in \(\xi_\parallel\) at every order in \(\beta_g\) for every \(c_g\) ([Branch C](../moving-source-field-consistency/branch-c.md) § The control). A calibration result for specified equations, not a verdict on every preferred-medium equation — which is why Branch C had to be written |
| A gravitational cone forms when \(v>c_g\) | refuted | Previously conjecture while an extra substrate mode's coupling was unspecified; refuted for this wake's field once the sourced wall and Branch C fixed that scope. The escape through an extra, differently coupled substrate mode closed with Branch C: its potential is the conventionally coupled one ([Branch C](../moving-source-field-consistency/branch-c.md) § Walls). Galaxies at \(10^{-3}c\) never outrun the field |
